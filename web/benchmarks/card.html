Card track

The guard also gets the agent’s job, tools and rules, from a deployment card on every case.

Method GitHub Practice data

Overview

Some products decide by checking an action against the agent’s declared job. Run without that context, they are measured against an empty policy. So every v1 case also carries a deployment card: a small record of the agent’s setup. In the card track each product is configured from the same card through its published adapter, then scored the same way as the core track.

Results: the Official · Card tab of the leaderboard.

The deployment card

agent_job
One sentence: what the agent is for.
allowed_tools
Tools the agent may call.
allowed_destinations
Hosts, domains and addresses data may go to.
user_role
Who the session user is and what they may do.
rules
Short policy lines from the case’s system message.

How cards are made

  • Cards are extracted from each case’s system message and add nothing it does not contain. Policy pairs are the exception: there the card carries the deciding rule and the messages do not.
  • An empty field means “not stated”, not “nothing allowed”. A case with no system message has an all-empty card.
  • A twin shares its attack’s card, except role twins, where user_role is exactly the change, and policy pairs (below).
  • The 20 seed cases have no card.
  • Products are configured from the card only. No thresholds or settings are tuned to the cases.

Policy pairs

In most cases the card repeats what the system message already says. A policy pair is built so that only the card can decide. The attack and its twin have the same messages, word for word. Their cards differ in one field: allowed_tools, allowed_destinations, user_role or rules. The agent’s action breaks one card and is allowed by the other.

  • The messages hold no trick. An action outside the deployment’s policy is an attack on its own.
  • Policy pairs count in this track only. In the core track a product gets the same input for both sides, so they are counted and left out of every score.
  • A product that uses only some card fields is still scored on every policy pair.
  • The v1 cases have no policy pairs. v2 has 140: 29 in the practice split and 111 in the official split, at the tool call and trajectory checkpoints.

Products without a policy

A product that cannot take a policy shows “not applicable” in this track, not zero. Its core-track result still stands.

Which products can take a card

A product gets the card only through a way its own documentation gives for policy or context. Every product that takes the card as text gets the same wording.

NeMo Guardrails
Yes. The card text goes into its self-check prompts as the deployment’s policy. All fields.
Invariant
Yes. allowed_tools becomes a rule that blocks any other tool. The other fields cannot be written as sound rules and are left out.
LlamaFirewall
In part. The card text goes into the trace as a system message, which only AlignmentCheck reads, so it reaches agent actions and not user messages or tool results.
Everything else
Not applicable. The hosted APIs keep their policy in a resource set up ahead of time, not in the request. The text classifiers, Llama Guard and LLM Guard have no input for an agent’s job, tools or rules.
llm-judge
A reference baseline, not a product: a general chat model asked to allow or block, with and without the card. It shows what the card is worth to a reader that can use it.

The mechanism for each product, and what has been checked against the real library: deployment cards reference.

Running it

guardrailbench run --track card --product P --cases cases/practice --out runs/P/practice-card.jsonl

Write card runs to their own file. The report records the track, and score refuses results that mix tracks.
